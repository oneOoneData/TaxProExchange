It's the phone call every California practitioner is about to get. A client, excited, says: "I'm buying my first EV and California is giving me $3,500 — do I claim that on my tax return?"

The answer is no. And how you explain it matters more than you'd think.

On August 7, Governor Newsom launched **MyFirstEV**, a state program that knocks **$3,500 off the price of a new zero-emission vehicle** — or **$1,750 off a used one** — at the dealership. No application. No waiting for refundable credits. No paperwork at tax time. The discount comes off the price before the client drives off the lot.

It's the biggest state-level EV incentive since the federal $7,500 credit died — and it's going to generate a wave of client questions. Here's what you need to know, and what to tell them.

## The program, in plain numbers

MyFirstEV is the product of **SB 168**, signed into law July 13, 2026, and managed by the California Air Resources Board (CARB). The state put up **$135.5 million**, and participating automakers matched it dollar-for-dollar — a combined **$271 million** in point-of-sale discounts.

The rules, per CARB:

- **$3,500 off** a new zero-emission vehicle with an MSRP **up to $50,000**
- **$1,750 off** a used ZEV sold for **up to $25,000** through manufacturers' certified pre-owned programs
- Open to any **California resident buying or leasing their first ZEV** — battery-electric or hydrogen fuel cell. **Hybrids don't count.**
- The rebate is **applied instantly at the dealership**. Buyers sign a legal declaration that this is their first EV purchase or lease.

A few wrinkles worth knowing:

- **It's first-timers only.** If a client previously bought *or leased* an EV — even a short lease years ago — they don't qualify. That's the fine print that catches people.
- **No income cap** on MyFirstEV itself. Unlike the old Clean Vehicle Rebate Project, eligibility doesn't phase out by income.
- **Brand rollout is staggered.** Live today: **Hyundai, Lucid, Tesla**. Coming in **August: Ford, Rivian, Chevrolet, Kia**. **September: Toyota/Lexus, Honda, Subaru**. **November: Mitsubishi**. Nissan and Volvo are still deciding.
- **California-headquartered automakers are exempt from the price cap.** That exception exists for manufacturers based in California — relevant if your client is shopping Lucid's higher-priced models.
- **Funds are divided equally among automakers, and they can run out.** If your client has their heart set on a specific brand, waiting too long can cost them the discount entirely.

## The tax reality: it's a rebate, not a credit

Here's where practitioners earn their fee. Clients will call this a "tax credit." It is not. There is no form to file, no line on the 1040, and no credit to carry forward. MyFirstEV is a **purchase price reduction** — economically identical to a dealer discount.

That has three consequences:

**1. It's not taxable income.** A reduction in purchase price is not income. Your client doesn't report the $3,500 as a rebate on their return, and they don't pay state or federal tax on it.

**2. It reduces basis — and therefore depreciation.** For clients who use the vehicle in a business (Schedule C, Schedule F, or inside an entity), depreciation — including bonus depreciation — is computed on the **net cost after the rebate**. A $48,000 EV with a $3,500 rebate has a depreciable basis of $44,500. Miss this and you're over-depreciating.

**3. Basis also matters for the eventual sale.** When the client sells or trades the vehicle, gain or loss is measured from the reduced basis. Keep the rebate paperwork in the file — it's support for the basis on the asset schedule.

## The federal context clients will confuse

Part of the confusion is timing. A year ago, a client could buy an EV and claim up to **$7,500** on their federal return. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act repealed the federal **new clean vehicle credit (Section 30D)** and the **used clean vehicle credit (Section 25E)** for vehicles acquired after **September 30, 2025**. The federal **commercial clean vehicle credit (Section 45W)** also ended for vehicles acquired after that date, with a narrow binding-contract exception.

So the $3,500 from California isn't replacing a federal credit the client can still get — the federal consumer credits are gone. MyFirstEV is the replacement, and it's better in one way: it's immediate. The old federal credit required waiting until filing season; this discount is in hand before the client leaves the dealership.

## Stacking: the planning opportunity

MyFirstEV doesn't exist in a vacuum. CARB confirms the discount can be **stacked with other incentive programs**, most notably:

- **Clean Cars 4 All** — for households at or below **300% of the federal poverty level**, worth **up to $12,000** in additional assistance
- **Driving Clean Assistance Program** — additional support for low-income buyers

For a qualifying client, that's potentially **$15,500+ in combined savings** on a first EV. If you work with lower-income families — or refer clients to community-based organizations — this is a real year-end planning conversation, not a marketing footnote.

## What to tell clients

When the call comes in — and it will — here's the script:

1. **"It's real, and it's automatic."** The discount happens at the dealership. No forms, no waiting for a refund. Bring a copy of the CARB program page if the salesperson is confused.
2. **"It's not on your tax return."** No credit to claim, nothing to report. It's a price discount, like a rebate from the manufacturer.
3. **"Only if it's your first EV."** A past EV purchase or lease disqualifies them — even a two-year-old lease. And hybrids don't qualify; it has to be battery-electric or hydrogen fuel cell.
4. **"Check the price cap and the brand timing."** New vehicles must be under $50,000 MSRP; used under $25,000. If their preferred brand isn't live yet, know the launch month — and that funds can run out.
5. **"If it's a business vehicle, tell me before you sign."** Because the rebate lowers basis, the purchase price on the contract isn't the number we depreciate from. We want the paperwork showing the rebate applied.

The bottom line: MyFirstEV is the biggest reason in years for a California client to finally go electric — and the biggest source of confusion we'll clean up this fall. Know the rules, correct the "tax credit" framing, and protect the basis. That's the job.
